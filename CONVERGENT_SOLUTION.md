# One new method: day-gated household personas

**Status: research and implementation plan only (2026-11-15).** No score is claimed for this method. A 10% improvement cannot be guaranteed before the hidden qualifying labels are scored. This plan specifies one structurally distinct experiment and a strict test for whether it closes the estimated gap.

## Target and evidence reviewed

**The success target is private-test RMSE ≤ 0.85725**, exactly 10% below Cinematch's 0.9525 private-test RMSE (0.9525 × 0.90). The best *promoted* result here is **R9-LAT-A2h, 0.8668706425** five-user-fold Probe development CV, using 79 prediction columns, 22 K1 latent/bias features, three LightGBM seeds and a 50/50 neural-blend average ([PROGRESS.md](PROGRESS.md), [ROUND9.md](ROUND9.md)). Probe development CV and private-test RMSE are different measurements. The Phase 0 rehearsal reports **0.00755** retraining gain and **−0.00018** CV-minus-transfer difference for a nine-column bank ([PHASE0_REHEARSAL.md](PHASE0_REHEARSAL.md), blend table and mapping). Its implied offset is **0.00755 − 0.00018 = 0.00737**. Applying that offset to the prize target gives **0.86462 Probe CV as a provisional planning proxy only** (0.85725 + 0.00737). The corresponding estimated development shortfall is **0.8668706425 − 0.86462 = 0.0022506425 RMSE**, or **0.0038970 MSE**. Neither 0.86462 nor a 0.0023 development gain is the success target. The offset was measured on a smaller bank and must be remeasured on the current one.

I reviewed the recorded baseline, temporal and joint factor models, SVD++, item/user neighborhoods, local GP and adaptive ridge, RBMs, AutoRec and DeepRecommender, masked-history and sequence models, Bayesian flipped factorization, metadata and rating-distribution features, latent/neighbor blend features, and the neural/LightGBM ensembles in [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md), [GAP_AUDIT.md](GAP_AUDIT.md), [MOONSHOTS.md](MOONSHOTS.md), [ROUND6.md](ROUND6.md), [ROUND7.md](ROUND7.md), [ROUND8.md](ROUND8.md), [ROUND9.md](ROUND9.md), and [ROUND10.md](ROUND10.md). Recent variants give diminishing returns: B5 lowered mean RMSE by only 0.0000495 and failed the all-fold promotion rule ([ROUND10.md](ROUND10.md)). The user-day factor J4 improved its standalone model modestly, then overfit after one epoch ([RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md)). The A1 item-side twin added essentially nothing ([GAP_AUDIT.md](GAP_AUDIT.md)). Another larger version of these methods is not an evidence-backed way to close the provisional development shortfall.

## The missing mechanism

An account may contain several people with distinct, **persistent** tastes. The proposed constraint is to reuse the *same few account-specific taste offsets across different dates* and infer their weights from the target day's available context. The closest recorded mechanisms differ:

| Recorded method | What the workspace shows | Missing from that method |
|---|---|---|
| J4 joint factors ([RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md); [src/joint.hpp](src/joint.hpp)) | Independent per-user-day factor vectors, with early overfit | A small, recurring set of account-specific persona vectors shared across days |
| C10/C11 day metadata ([SESSION_META.md](SESSION_META.md)) | Same-day training-rating summaries and joint residual summaries as blend features | Learned recurring taste vectors and a gate that chooses among them |
| HN3 and MT1/sequence session context ([HISTORY_EXPERIMENTS.md](HISTORY_EXPERIMENTS.md); [ROUND7.md](ROUND7.md); [scripts/masked_sequence.py](scripts/masked_sequence.py)) | Date-local history weighting or a session-conditioned neural code | Explicit reuse of a few account-specific personas across dates |
| Local residual factorization ([src/moonshot_local.cpp](src/moonshot_local.cpp); [MOONSHOTS.md](MOONSHOTS.md)) | Sparse experts routed by movie-factor clusters, independently of the target day | Day-context inference over recurring personas within an account |

This inventory supports **untried in the recorded workspace**, not global novelty or a proof that the mechanism will improve RMSE. J4's unconstrained user-day factors are the nearest parameterization; sharing a few offsets across days is the testable difference.

Primary-research motivation is [*Guess Who Rated This Movie?* (UAI 2012)](https://www.auai.org/uai2012/papers/144.pdf), which studies identification of multiple raters within a shared account, and [Gomez-Uribe and Hunt (2015)](https://gwern.net/doc/reinforcement-learning/exploration/2015-gomezuribe.pdf), which discusses multiple viewers using one profile. [BellKor's time and neighborhood work](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=f4ebb542c752a0dc423f94fd121e2edb8f6275ba&repid=rep1&type=pdf) motivates using date and nearby-rating context. These papers are **motivation, not evidence of a competition-split gain for this model**. The UAI paper's household classifications are not ground-truth prevalence or a forecast of this model's accuracy, so the plan does not use them to predict a gain.

## Proposed model

Fit a **small mixture of recurring account personas**, with a gate inferred from *other observed ratings on the target day* and from the target movie's identity. Start from the frozen K1 item geometry and the strongest available base prediction, rather than training another full rating model. For account `u`, let `p_u` be its existing effective K1 factor and let `δ_{u,1}`, `δ_{u,2}` be regularized persona offsets. For movie `i` with frozen factor `q_i`, predict a correction

```
z_k(u,i,t) = q_i · δ_{u,k} + b_{u,k}
w_k(u,i,t) = softmax_k(g_k(movie i, date t, other observed movies on day t))
prediction = frozen_base(u,i,t) + shrink(u,t) * Σ_k w_k z_k
```

The gate uses the **movie IDs** of all known same-day interactions, including unlabeled probe/qualifying pairs under the repo's existing transductive protocol, and the **rating values only of training rows** on that day. For a training target, exclude its own rating and movie event from its gate input. For a probe/qualifying target, exclude its unknown rating, while allowing its movie ID as the query. A simple first gate combines (a) the similarity of the query movie to each persona, (b) a pooled embedding of other same-day movie IDs, and (c) a pooled, target-excluded residual from other same-day *training* ratings. A learned or EM-style gate can follow only if the simple gate wins. The correction gets strong ridge shrinkage, with the shrink factor increasing only when an account has enough history and the day supplies enough context. Accounts with insufficient evidence receive zero correction.

This is **one method**, with internal controls. It adapts the household-mixture insight to this repository's dated rating data and existing factor/ensemble bank. It is deliberately an additive residual predictor: the current 79-column ensemble retains its complementary signals, and the new model can be rejected without rebuilding them.

## Why it might close the gap, and what would falsify it

The provisional proxy requires about **0.00390 reduction in average squared error**, about **0.52%** of the current 0.86687² MSE. If the method affects 25% of scored rows and leaves other rows unchanged, it would need approximately **0.0156 MSE reduction on affected rows**. This is a demanding, unmeasured requirement. The opportunity is high-history accounts with same-day context; gains may be small if those accounts are rare in Probe, if the sequence model already captures the context, or if the proposed personas merely fit noise.

The decisive development test is **incremental ensemble gain on the same rows and folds**, not a better standalone persona score or a cherry-picked subgroup. Require improvement in all five held-out-user folds. Use the provisional 0.0023 RMSE shortfall to size the experiment, then remeasure the transfer offset on the current bank and ask whether the resulting **estimated private-test RMSE is ≤ 0.85725**. A development score around 0.86462 by itself does not establish success. Freeze the recipe and train on train+probe for qualifying predictions only after this check. **The sole success criterion is private-test RMSE ≤ 0.85725**; without hidden labels, report a calibrated estimate and its uncertainty rather than a verified 10% result.

## Implementation sequence (no implementation has started)

1. **Freeze the comparison.** Hash the R9-LAT-A2h prediction bank, fold IDs, blender settings, and current best out-of-fold column. Resolve the incomplete R10-KNNF result before using it as a control. Use only the original Probe split and keep its labels out of persona fitting.
2. **Build a training-only feasibility screen.** Using K1 item vectors and training ratings, measure the fraction of Probe rows whose accounts have at least 100 and 500 training ratings, and whose target day has other known movies or training ratings. Estimate the maximum overall gain achievable if only these rows change. If too few rows are eligible to supply the provisional 0.00390 MSE reduction under reasonable subgroup errors, stop before model training.
3. **Fit two personas with frozen geometry.** For eligible users, optimize two offset vectors and biases by regularized weighted least squares/EM on training residuals against a *fixed* base model. Initialize offsets by splitting rating residuals along the leading item-factor direction; keep a one-person solution and penalize the second persona. Use an inner train-only split to select regularization, minimum history and gate settings. Do not use in-sample residuals from an overfit base model without checking them against its training-only held-out residuals.
4. **Test the mechanism.** Emit a full Probe-order float32 correction/prediction column and support features. Run ablations with exactly the same data and compute: one persona; two personas without day context; two personas with movie IDs but no same-day rating values; and the full day-gated model. A true household signal should improve especially on high-history/context rows and survive the held-out-user folds. The one-person and ungated controls show whether the gain is merely extra capacity.
5. **Measure ensemble gain.** Add only the predeclared full-model column and support features to the frozen R9 bank. Use the same `round4_boost.py` three LightGBM seeds, `round8_nn_blend.py` three neural seeds, nested user folds and fixed 50/50 average. Compare paired fold RMSE and per-user squared-error deltas against R9-LAT-A2h; inspect whether gains persist across dates, activity bins and all five folds. Record hashes, source snapshot, training time and failure modes with `scripts/experiment.py`.
6. **Recalibrate against the 0.85725 target and prepare Stage C only if the gate passes.** Repeat the Phase 0 P2 rehearsal with the new component and current-bank representatives, using fixed hyperparameters, to measure the new train+probe offset. Generate qualifying predictions only after the model and blend are frozen. Report the inferred private-test score as an estimate, not a verified result.

**Stop rules.** Stop after the feasibility screen if eligible coverage makes the needed overall MSE gain implausible. Stop the persona fit if it fails to improve P2 over the one-person control. Do not spend more on a complex gate if the simple two-person model shows no paired gain. Do not promote a Probe result that worsens any held-out-user fold, even if its mean improves. These gates prevent another expensive moonshot from being mistaken for progress.
