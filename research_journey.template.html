Can AI win the $1M Netflix Challenge

Twenty years ago, in October 2006, Netflix offered $1 million to cut Cinematch’s 0.9525 Test RMSE by 10%, to about 0.8572. Rival teams BellKor, BigChaos, and Pragmatic Theory joined forces as BellKor’s Pragmatic Chaos. After nearly three years and a close finish against The Ensemble, their July 2009 submission won the prize, which Netflix awarded that September. Read Netflix’s announcement.

My experiment: can Codex and Claude match the competition's 10% improvement? I use them to build bias, factor, neighbor, RBM, and sequence models from Netflix's training ratings, then blend their predictions. Probe labels stay out of base-model training. I score each blend with five user-disjoint Probe folds: fit on four and predict the fifth, so every Probe rating gets an out-of-fold prediction. R10's 0.8668212 RMSE is my lowest measured blend, but two folds worsened, so R9's 0.8668706 remains promoted. These are development scores because I used Probe feedback to choose what to promote. Netflix never released the hidden Test ratings, so I cannot calculate an official prize score for my models.

Why? The Netflix Challenge has a special place in my heart. I have fond memories participating in the competition and following its progress. I still come back to it from time to time, long after the competition ended. It compelled me to learn how to build my own computer because my 512 MB XPS could not handle the amount of data. Even though the majority of my time was spent learning memory management in C and waiting days for computation results, I was super happy when my submissions scored 1.02 and 0.97. Neither beat Cinematch. The idea of finally reaching the winning mark on my own machine makes this return especially rewarding.

03 / The playbook

What actually moved the needle

Success came from adding different kinds of information, then checking whether the gain held across users. Each strategy left a record of method, result and limitation.

Foundation · Sep 27

Biases → time → factors

Regularized user/movie biases reached 0.98257. Temporal and frequency effects took the single-model score to 0.93492; 32-factor SVD++ reached 0.90267. Joint factor training later became the stronger backbone.

Lesson: time and interaction both matter.
Local structure · Sep 27

Neighbors break the factor ceiling

Learned item neighborhoods and residual kNN added movie relationships the shared latent space missed. B4 fell to 0.88666; expanding to 128 neighbors gave B7 0.88585.

Lesson: standalone rank is not ensemble value.
Ensemble · Sep 27–28

Mix banks, then correct errors

LightGBM combined factor, neighbor, RBM and AutoRec columns at 0.88040. A fixed cross-branch average reached 0.87822. Residual trees, wider kNN banks and metadata then drove R5-meta to 0.87481.

Lesson: diversity plus controlled blending pays.
Generative signal · Sep 28

Conditional RBMs

Six conditional RBM columns with differing seeds and hidden sizes moved the three-seed blend to 0.87312. Later near-duplicate columns brought tiny or inconsistent gains.

Lesson: architecture diversity beat another epoch.
Long histories · Sep 30–Oct 9

Masked sequence models

MH3 and MT variants crossed 0.871. Bayesian factors and longer masked histories helped in Round 8. A2h's 1536-token context with LightGBM + NN2 reached 0.86710 across all five folds.

Lesson: user history adds signal beyond scalar metadata.
Final promoted gain · Oct 10

Expose latent features to the blender

R9 gave the tree and neural blender 22 frozen K1 latent and bias features on the existing 79-column bank. The fixed average reached 0.8668706, with every fold improved.

Lesson: better features can beat more raw predictors.

Parallel history, GP and session work

Quan-only commits recorded rating-history pilots on Sep 28, a visible-context RBM pilot on Sep 30, then compact GP and session-context blends. The branch moved from C5 0.8759028 to C12 0.8747781 development CV. C12 narrowly beat historical R5 overall; the main Round 7 bank had already moved further. History pilots · session work.

Promising mean, failed gate

R10-B5's item-frequency Bayesian column reached 0.8668212, but two folds worsened. It was measured and kept as a screen, not promoted.

Interesting, weaker alone

The BAR paper port scored 0.9741851 Probe RMSE in its tested configuration. It improved its own baseline but did not challenge the ensemble.

Still open

Round 10 extracted kNN neighbor features for the blenders. The recorded branch launched a follow-up chain; no completed blend score was in the merged record.

04 / Branch integration

Ten research branches, one main

The October 11 Codex work unified the parallel experiments without erasing the branch histories. That operation preserved the score trail and made the methods easier to inspect; it did not run a new scoring experiment.

Fetch and merge

Keep every branch

All 11 remote refs (10 research branches plus origin/main) were fetched. Their histories were merged into main; every original branch still exists. The method and score status of each branch is recorded in MERGE_RECORD.md.

Resolve overlaps

Combine compatible code

Shared build rules and CLI commands were reconciled. The richer sequence and Bayesian implementations were retained. Alternate RBM checkpoint formats required a documented choice, while the paper branch's version remains in its branch history.

Verify and preserve

Tests and provenance

The merged C++ build, core tests, push checks, and masked-history tests passed. Author and committer emails were rewritten as requested; commit dates, file trees, messages and Claude co-author trailers were verified unchanged. Commit IDs necessarily changed.
